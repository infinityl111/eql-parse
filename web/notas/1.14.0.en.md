## This version re-reads your log, and it changes where each fight starts and ends

It goes first because it is what happens the moment you open it: the first
launch takes a while, because the whole log is re-read once.

Nothing can be corrected while reading, and not for lack of trying: **the
boundary between two fights is decided at the moment they are written**, so the
only way to apply the new model to what you already have is to go back to the
log. Nothing is lost — `fights.ndjson` comes from the log, which is the source —
but **your history is divided up differently**.

## You are coming from 1.12.0, and 1.13.0 comes inside this one

1.13.0 never got published: its code exists and its notes exist, but it never had
an installer, so nobody has it. Its fixes arrive **inside this one**, and there
are six:

- **the time you were not in control stops counting against you.** A fear or a
  charm leaves the log without a single line of yours, and that was being read as
  you having wandered off. In your history that is 31 episodes and 251 seconds
  charged to you that you could not avoid
- **a charmed groupmate stops being an enemy.** One hit of theirs was enough to
  move them to the other side with all their damage behind them; in the worst
  fight, 55 % of the "{{metric.enemyTotal}}" was your own group
- **"{{inc.title}}"**, which gathers what a fight cannot know: damage with no
  owner, damage counted by deduction, and loot with two possible corpses
- **"{{rp.pista.title}}"** in the replay, with what happened to you during the
  fight and how long each thing lasted
- **"{{foe.youResist}}"** per enemy and per spell, instead of one loose total
- and the fights that **a fear used to cut in two**

The full 1.13.0 notes are on its own version page. What follows is this one's.

## We were answering a different question

Until now, "is this the same fight?" was answered with "how long has the log been
quiet?". Those are two different questions, and that is why it failed in both
directions at once:

- **it cut a fight in two** because you spent twenty-five seconds clearing adds
- **and it merged two** because the next one started three seconds after the
  first

Both came from the same place: a gap of silence says nothing about who is
fighting you.

## What a fight is, now

**A fight is a group of enemies whose participation windows overlap.** Each enemy
has one window, from its first line to its last; as long as one is open, the
fight continues.

Put the way it looks from inside the game: five enemies killed in two minutes,
all hitting or casting at once, their names appearing interleaved in the log, are
**one** fight. Five killed in two minutes but one at a time, with none of them
doing anything while you attack the previous one, are **five**.

An enemy that dies closes its window **immediately**: a death is a measurement,
not a wait. Only the enemies you get away from need a grace period, and that
period is twelve seconds — the 99th percentile of the attack cadence measured in
your own log. Of the 19,970 silence gaps of two seconds or more, 99.1 % are
shorter than that: they are not a mystery, they are the mob's rhythm.

## A sleeping enemy no longer breaks the fight

A mez produces no lines at all: the mob goes quiet and looks like it left. But
the log **does write** that it is asleep, and both ways of waking up.

Those lines were not being read. Your log has **217 mez landings** and **353
closing lines** — "has been awakened by" and "spell has worn off of" — that went
into the unrecognised bin. With them, 202 of the 217 have a window with a written
beginning and end.

Now silence explained by a measured state — a mez, a charm, or a stretch where
you were not in control of your character — **closes no window**. And every fight
that leans on that keeps a record of **which** line is holding it up, over which
enemy, and how much silence it covered, so it can be argued with instead of
believed.

What is not there: root. In 850,171 lines there is not one saying an enemy is
rooted, so it cannot be measured and it is not invented.

## And that is why your figures stop being diluted

This is what you notice when you open a fight, and it is not tidiness in the
list.

A twenty-seven-minute pull with forty mobs that die in one hit had a
"{{metric.raidDps}}" divided by all the time you spent walking between them. The
number was correct and described nothing. Now each pull has its own figure, and
"{{det.dpsFight}}" means what it says again.

The same goes for "{{metric.duration}}", "{{an.downtime}}" and
"{{det.dpsActive}}": a denominator holding three fights and two walks measures
nobody.

## A death no longer founds a fight

A death is not a beginning: it is proof of something that was already happening.
If the death of an enemy arrives that has not produced a single line in the open
fight — a poison finishing on its own, or someone else landing the last blow — it
no longer counts as your kill: it is recorded separately, together with how far
back its last line was.

In your history that is **21 deaths** out of more than four thousand. It is few,
and it is said anyway: it was a kill that was not yours, in the title of a fight
that was not its own.

## How much your history changes

On the reference log, saved fights go from **845 to 1,420**, and the median
duration drops from **86 to 45 seconds**. The number you end up with depends on
how much you have played before rebuilding; the difference does not.

**But it does not change evenly.** In the Plane of Fear, the Plane of Hate,
Nagafen's Lair and the Plane of Sky — where you fight one thing at a time —
**83 % of the fights come out exactly the same**, and 457 become 536. What really
gets divided up are the pulls: the places where you chained one after another
without a pause, which is where a single row was hiding half an hour.

## What this model does, said precisely

**It only cuts, except where the log says in as many words that the enemy was
still there.** On rebuilding, every new fight fits inside one you already had —
with **ten exceptions** out of 845, and all ten can point at the mez or charm
line that joins them. Not one of them is joined by a gap of time.

## What can only be checked by playing

A fight closes through two paths that do not use the same clock: the log
timestamp and the wall clock. Re-reading is one of the two, so the only way to
check that they agree is to play a session and re-read it afterwards.

The wall-clock margin goes from three to five seconds because of this change —
the new grace period is shorter, so a lag costs more — which means a fight takes
five seconds to be called finished on the overlay instead of three. It affects no
figure: only when it appears.
