## What your log already said and we were not reading

Your log writes a line every time an enemy resists one of your spells. The
program did not know how to read it. On a real log that is **955 resists of
yours** thrown away, and with them the answer to a question that does change
what you do: *does this spell land on this creature?*

## The first time you open it, it asks to re-read your log

This version **asks you to rebuild your history**: on opening, a banner comes up
with two buttons, "Rebuild history" and "Not now". You can leave it for later,
but until you do, your saved fights still have no resists in them.

While it re-reads your log from the beginning, **you can keep using the
program**: the window does not freeze, you can switch sections and everything
moves normally. The only thing that stands still are the figures, until it
finishes.

**How long it takes.** Measured on a 103.8 MB log: **28 seconds** if the file is
fresh in the system, **62** if it has to come off the disk. It happens once.
What you have now is set aside with a timestamp before anything is touched.

**Why it is needed.** It is not that your saved fights have a low figure: it is
that the row does not exist. A spell that is **always** resisted never lands,
and without landing it never created its entry. The clearest case in a real log:
against one enemy, **62 casts, 62 resisted, not one landed** — and not one of
those 62 lines was stored anywhere. That cannot be fixed by reading the saved
data better; you have to go back to the log.

## How many times you were resisted, and out of how many tries

In each fight's analysis, per spell and against whom, **always with the
denominator**:

```
Drain Spirit X          against Coercer T`vala   44 of 44   never lands
Blade Dance             against Coercer T`vala   26 of 26   never lands
Water Elemental Attack  against Coercer T`vala    5 of 31   16%
…
```

"44 resisted" says nothing: 44 of 44 and 44 of 300 are two different fights and
two different decisions. And when something does not land even once, it is said
in words — **never lands** — and not only with a percentage you have to subtract.

Above the table it says **how many spells it comes from**: "of the 7 you cast
against this enemy, 5 were resisted at least once". Without that line, five rows
read as though you had cast five things.

## And it tells you out loud, which is what the checkbox promised

The **"a spell of yours resisted"** checkbox has existed since the first
version. **It had never once fired**: zero times across the 1.19 million lines
the program did recognise. Anyone who had it ticked had been waiting months for
a notice that could not arrive.

It works now, with two modes you choose:

- **One every 20 seconds per spell** — the one that comes set.
- **Every one**, if you would rather hear them all.

It is throttled by default for a measured reason: in that same fight there were
**103 resists out of 129 casts**, and 4 of the 7 spells never landed once.
"Every one" there means talking over the whole fight.

## Two figures on the row that read alike without being alike

The big number for each combatant is a **rate** — their damage divided by how
long the fight lasted — and the percentage beside it is a **share** — how much
of the total they put in. They are divided by different things and answer
different questions, and they sat side by side with nothing saying which was
which. Now they say so.

**The big number has not changed.** It is still damage over fight duration,
which is what dps means.

And their **own window** moves up to the visible line: damage divided by the
time that combatant spent in the fight. The window runs **from the first thing
they do or that is done to them, to the last**: hitting, missing, healing,
absorbing — and being hit — all count, so for whoever is tanking it opens on the
first blow they take, not on the first they land. That is the one that answers
"did they join late?" — someone arriving halfway reads low on the rate and
normal on their window, and with one figure those two cases are
indistinguishable. It only shows when it differs.
