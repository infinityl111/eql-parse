## Your saved fights are going to show different numbers. Without you pressing anything

This comes first because it is what will happen the moment you open the app.

**1.10.0 asked your permission: a notice appeared, there was a button, and your
previous history was set aside with a timestamp before anything was touched.
This one does not ask.** The correction happens on its own as each fight is
read, so the fights you already had will show different figures the first time
you open them.

There is nothing to decide and nothing is lost: your `fights.ndjson` is not
touched, the correction happens while reading it. But a history that changes
without warning is exactly what this program does not do, so here is the
warning. It also appears inside the application, once.

## What was wrong

**Stance does not reduce damage over time or damage shields, and the program
was acting as if it did.**

The log stores damage already mitigated, so in order to compare stances against
each other the program has to reconstruct what you would have taken with none.
That was also being done to damage over time and to damage shields, and
measured over a 385,656-line log, stance does not touch them:

- Nine applications of incoming damage over time cross a stance change with the
  spell already on you. In all nine the tick value does not move by a single
  point. `Rotting Flesh` from a dracoliche crosses Defensive ↔ Channeler in
  both directions and is worth 100 across all 42 ticks.
- Damage shields, the same: a spite golem pricks for 18 under Defensive, under
  Channeler and under Offensive.

**Direct spells behave differently — they are mitigated, and with textbook
precision.** `Soul Devour` from the Eye of Veeshan hits for 400 under
Defensive, 300 under Channeler and 250 under Mage Hunter, across 368 hits and
without a single variation. So this was never about giving up on reconstructing
magic damage: they were two schools measured separately with two different
results.

**What it cost:** 44,924 points of incoming damage that never existed, across
416 saved fights. And the stance advice was resting on them — 21% of the damage
was being counted as avoidable when no stance avoids it.

## What changes in what you see

- **Incoming damage goes down** in fights that had damage over time or damage
  shields. That is the figure that was inflated.
- **The advice panel has a new row**: how much of the damage that reached you
  no stance was stopping. It is the ceiling on everything the table below
  promises, so now it is in plain sight.
- **The verdict stops pretending you held a single stance.** If you switched
  stance during the fight, what you avoided is now worked out band by band.
  Before, it took whichever stance lasted longest and credited it with the
  whole fight's damage, and in a fight where you switch around that can invert
  the advice: it could tell you to change to a stance that would have avoided
  *less* than what you actually did.
- **The "Wrong stance in places" finding in the analysis moves.** Phases are cut when
  the composition of incoming damage changes, and damage over time was counting
  as magic: a melee fight with a poison ticking on you was cut as if the boss
  had switched to burning you.

## Your history will end up mixed, and that is correct

Not everything can be corrected by reading. What can: the observed amount is
stored alongside the reconstructed one, so undoing the improper reconstruction
is an exact copy and happens on its own.

What cannot: the split of damage by the stance you were in for each hit **is
not on disk**. That has to come from re-reading the whole log, and today that
cannot be asked for with any guarantee (see below).

Practical consequence: **in your older fights you will see "fight saved before
damage was split by stance"**, and in the new ones you will not. That is
correct and it is not a bug: it is the program telling you that this particular
verdict is worked out against a single stance because that is all that can be
done with what is stored. Every fight now carries a number saying which rules
its figures were worked out under.

## Rebuilding now warns you by itself

If you use `store:rebuild`, or the rebuild button, you will see a new warning:
**rebuilding does not reproduce your history exactly.**

A fight is closed using the wall clock while the program is running, and using
the log's own timestamp when it is rebuilt. Wherever the gap between two fights
is exactly 20 seconds, each path decides differently. Measured over 441 real
fights: one merges with the next, and both fights' figures change with it.

The previous state is still set aside with a timestamp and can be recovered.
But it is better to know before you press than after. **This is a known bug and
it is pending a fix**; until then, this version does not ask you to rebuild.

## And a piece of plumbing that showed

Until now a single number answered two questions: "has what gets stored
changed?" and "does the log have to be re-read?". For thirteen versions
running, both answers were the same, so nobody noticed they were two questions.
This is the first version where they disagree.

They are separated now. The consequence is that the test which stops a format
change being published without numbering it **no longer has any exceptions**:
before, saying "the format changed but don't rebuild" meant silencing it.

## Release notes, inside the application

The update notice has been carrying this text since 1.10.0 and was not showing
it anywhere. Now it shows it, folded, before you decide to install. Somebody
updating from inside the app should not have to go to the website to read what
is about to change.
## Going back costs nothing, and it is not an emergency procedure

The correction lives in the code that reads, not in the file: your
`fights.ndjson` keeps the old numbers intact. If you install 1.10.0 over this
one, **the fights from before you updated go back to showing exactly what they
showed**, with no backups to restore and nothing to undo.

And the ones you record with this version are not left behind either. Measured
over 440 fights written by 1.11.0 and read by 1.10.0: **not one error, not one
fight losing its advice, not one point of damage lost.** Incoming damage comes
out identical —3,374,795 on both— and the phase analysis gives exactly the same:
1,820 phases and 183 stance findings on both, zero fights differing.

The one thing the old version cannot do is separate the third bucket: it puts
damage over time and damage shields inside "magic", because it only has two. The
damage is all there, counted in one drawer fewer. The consequence is that it
goes back to counting that damage as avoidable when scoring stances, which is
its usual model.

The only thing it does on opening is rebuild its own encyclopedia, which takes
under a second and is its normal behaviour.
