## This version rebuilds your history, and this time it doesn't ask

First, because it's what happens the moment you open it.

1.11.0 corrected while reading, which is why it asked for nothing. This one
can't: three of the things it fixes **were decided when each fight was written**,
and what was decided at write time cannot be recomposed by reading. So your
whole log is read again, once. Nothing is lost — `fights.ndjson` is regenerated
from the log, which is the source — but it takes a while, and your fights will
change numbers and some will change boundaries.

The three things that can only be fixed by rebuilding are the first three on
this list.

## Time you weren't in control of no longer counts against you

When an enemy fears you, you run, and you can't do anything. Under a charm you
can't either: you hit your own group, but you don't choose to. In both cases the
log goes without a single combat line of yours, and the program was reading that
as if you had stopped fighting.

Those spans are now recognised by both ends, measured, and **taken out of every
figure that talks about you**: idle time, the denominator of your active dps,
the stance verdict and the swing cadence.

Measured on your log: 31 episodes, and **251 seconds you were being charged for
and could not avoid**. The worst fight goes from 172 to 91 seconds of idle time.

Two things were corrected along the way, and they ran in both directions: short
fear gaps were *forgiving* you too much in the cadence — that goes too, because
the rule is neither for nor against. And while you had no control they kept
hitting you, so the denominator of your active dps grew while the numerator
couldn't.

The label says **feared** or **charmed** by measurement, not by resemblance: if
you did absolutely nothing inside the span, it's fear; if you did something and
it was against your own side, it's charm. One case had to be told apart or it
would have ruined this: keys the game refuses. Inside those spans there are 24
casts that begin and never resolve — no landing, no resist, no interrupt.
Outside them, that same spell does resolve. A cast with no outcome isn't an
action, it's a refused attempt, and counting it would have turned 10 of the 31
episodes into something else because of a hammered hotkey.

## A charmed groupmate is no longer an enemy

If someone in your group gets charmed and hits you, until now they became an
enemy **for the whole fight**, not just for the exchange. Sides were deduced from
who hits whom, and one blow was enough.

Five of your fights had friendly fire, all in the Plane of Fear. In three of
them a groupmate ended up on the opposing side, and in the worst one **55% of
the "enemy damage" was your own group**. Your own figures lost out too: the
damage that groupmate did to the boss didn't count as yours.

What you declared now wins: someone you have said is on your side does not
change sides because a blow says otherwise. And damage exchanged among your own
is added to no total — it is set aside and counted, by name, in both directions.

The replay shows the span with what is known and nothing more: which groupmate
fought the group, how many blows, how much damage, and between which seconds.
The cause is not stated, because the log does not state it.

## What this fight doesn't know

For several versions the program has been working out how much of a fight it
can't vouch for, and showing it nowhere. Three numbers, measured, stored and
silent.

They are now together and visible, because they are the same question: real
damage with no owner, damage attributed on the strength of a deduction rather
than a line, and loot whose assignment is ambiguous. Each marked as measured or
deduced.

There is no new calculation here. The only change is that it is shown.

## The state track, in the replay

Below the timeline there is now a track with what happened to you during the
fight: stuns, charms, spans with no control, absorbs and knockdowns.

Where the duration is measured, a bar is drawn from start to end. Where it
wouldn't be distinguishable at the width you have on screen, a dot is drawn, and
widening the window turns dots into bars. That is deliberate: the track shows
the resolution that exists, not one it doesn't have. A state that began before
the fight, or never closed, is drawn open on that side.

Not every possible state is there, only the ones that say something. Root is
read and stored but not drawn: it is short and explains no gap.

## What you resist, per enemy

What you resist is no longer a loose per-fight total. It now lives on each
enemy's card, along with which of its spells it was — which is what actually
helps you decide what gear to bring back to that place.

## Fights that were split in two

A long enough span with no control cut a fight in two, because during fear there
is no line of yours at all and that read as the fight having ended. It happened
through both paths, the log's and the wall clock's.

It no longer cuts. Across your history, **714 fights become 710**: five merge
with their neighbour and one moves its boundary.

## Still not closed

Rebuilding does not reproduce your history exactly. Of the ten measured
differences between what the wall clock decides and what the log's timestamp
decides, seven are closed. The remaining three are application starts in the
middle of a log, and no clock rule splits them.

It's said here because it's better to know before rebuilding than to find out
afterwards.
