## Timers, in one panel over the game

One panel, all of them inside. It opens as soon as you open the first and
closes when you close the last — nothing to remember.

- sorted by **the one that comes back soonest**, and the ones already due, on top
- one line per timer: name, time, and **how many gaps** hold up its ceiling
- the **×** on a line closes that timer, not the window

And if you're in a zone where you have none, it **doesn't go blank**: it tells
you how many you have elsewhere.

## Three states, and the third one was missing

A timer that **hasn't yet seen its creature die even once** doesn't say «it
should be there by now»: it says **«{{cro.esperando}}»**. Saying something ought
to be back when we don't even know when it left is making it up.

And it isn't a rare case: it's the state every timer starts in when opened from
the overlay.

## Set a timer from the overlay

Expand an enemy in the main overlay and the button is there. **It doesn't have
to be dead** — in fact it will usually be alive and hitting you, which is
exactly when you remember you want to track it.

The name, the zone and the difficulty come from where you are. Nothing to type.

## Every overlay resizes and dims

Drag from any of the **four corners**. And hovering brings up a **transparency
slider**.

Each overlay remembers **its own** position, size and transparency, so you
adjust one and the other stays as you left it.
