## This version asks you to rebuild your history, and rebuilding moves it

This comes first because you need to know it **before** pressing, not after.

When you open the app a notice will ask you to re-read your log. It is required
this time: where each item was attached was decided **when it was written**, and
there is no way to recompose it by reading what is stored more carefully.

**And rebuilding does not reproduce your history exactly.** A fight is closed
using the wall clock while the program is running and using the log's own
timestamp when it is re-read, so wherever the gap between two fights is exactly
20 seconds, each path decides differently. Measured on a real history of 708
fights: **700 remain — 698 identical, 10 that merge into the fight next to them
and 2 that come out split a different way.**

What you had **is set aside with a timestamp before anything is touched**
(`fights.ndjson`, the index, the encyclopedia and the side files) and can be
recovered by copying it back. Nothing is deleted.

This is a known bug, the same one 1.11.0 already warned about, and it is still
unfixed. The difference is that that version did not ask you to rebuild and this
one does.

## What was wrong with loot

**It was attached to whichever fight was open when you picked it up, and you
loot after killing.** Four things come out of that, all measured on a real 55 MB
log:

- **34 items attached to a fight later** than the death of the creature they
  came from. Always forward, never backward, with a median of ten minutes
  between one fight and the other. You kill, you move on, and ten minutes later
  you empty the corpse: the item went to whichever fight you were in.
- **294 items whose moment falls outside their own fight, and were therefore
  invisible.** The replay runs from zero to the fight's duration, so an item
  picked up after the last blow was never drawn anywhere. That is a third of the
  fights with loot.
- **51 items landed somewhere different** depending on whether they were
  recorded live or by re-reading — and they were precisely the boss ones,
  because a boss has ten items and emptying its corpse takes longer than the
  twenty seconds the fight needs to close.
- **One loot line with no rule.** `and stored it in your tradeskill depot`
  appeared once in the whole log and took a whole item with it without anything
  going off.

And **coin from corpses was not stored anywhere**: 1,392 lines the program
recognised and threw away.

## What changes

**Each item now goes to the fight where its corpse died**, not to whichever was
open. Over the 1,901 loot lines of the reference log: 1,884 end up attached to
the fight where the creature they came from died, 17 stay loose — because
someone else finished that enemy off entirely and that death was never recorded
— and none is lost.

The match is **by name and by closeness**, so it is a deduction and it is said
to be one: the log does not number corpses. What is measured is the gap between
the death and the pickup, and it appears beside each item. In the three pickups
out of 1,901 where two corpses were equally close in different fights, the doubt
is marked instead of choosing in silence.

**The replay now ends in a new card: "What you picked up".** Which items, from
which corpse, at what moment, ordered by the instant of pickup. The ones you
took with the fight already closed appear there, with their clock time and said
outright that the fight had ended — it is the first time they can be seen.

It is not titled "what dropped", and that is not an oversight: the log **does
not record what anyone else picks up**, nor what someone loots from a distant
corpse, nor what was left on the floor. It records what you took, and the card
is titled accordingly.

**And a fight with no loot no longer says one single thing**, because they are
not the same:

- enemies fell and no pickup of yours is recorded
- nothing died, so there was nothing to loot
- you picked it up, but outside any fight of yours

**Coin finally counts**, with its moment and summed in copper. It hangs off
whichever fight was open rather than off a corpse, because the log line does not
say whose it is — and that is written on the card too, next to the figure. They
are two different kinds of certainty and they are not shown as if they were one.

## And a net for next time

The missing line ending cost an item and **nobody saw it**: the theory was that
a line with no rule shows up in the unrecognised counter, but that counter has
39,000 lines in it and one loot line is indistinguishable from anything.

Now an unknown ending **still yields the item and the corpse**, and the part
that cannot be read travels through, literally, to the card. `store:check` names
it, which is where you look when something smells wrong.
