## The first time you open it, it re-reads your whole log

This version **forces a rebuild**. You will see a notice and a progress bar: it
is reading your log from the beginning to rebuild your history. It takes **a few
seconds** —measured, 25 s for 75 MB— and it happens **once**.

Nothing is deleted: the previous files are set aside with a timestamp before
anything is touched, and they can be restored.

**Why it is needed.** This version changes the name stored for each enemy, and
the evidence for how it is spelled lives in your log, not in the fights already
saved. Re-reading fixes the whole history; without it, only what comes next.

## The replay no longer draws fights that are not yours

Replaying a fight in a busy zone showed figures that were not in your fight:
another player's mob, the player, and the blows between them — drawn in their own
column as if they were part of yours.

The worst one in the history: a 99-second fight with **three combatants** that
drew **thirteen figures**, with 468 unrelated blows inside.

Measured over 1,493 fights: **446 drew someone from outside, and 49 drew entire
unrelated fights**. Now it is **190 and zero**. The 190 that remain are the
legitimate ones: someone who healed you, or who missed your mob, and was
therefore there.

## And it no longer greys everyone out on the first second

When an enemy went down, its figure was switched off **at second zero** of the
replay instead of at the second it died. With several enemies, the scene started
with everyone grey and stayed that way.

It happened in **80 %** of fights. Now each figure goes out when it fell.

## Names are spelled the way the game spells them

`The Prophet`, `The Spiroc Lord`, `The Muglwump` and two more were shown with a
lowercase article we had put there. EverQuest capitalises the first letter of
every line, so the only way to know a name's real spelling is to look at how the
game writes it **mid-sentence** — which is what happens now.

**And when we do not know, it says so.** Some names you have only ever seen at
the start of a line; for those, the spelling is our guess. They carry a note
saying exactly that, next to the combatant's row.

## «2 slain» is not the same as «at least 3 present»

A fight was titled `a shin ghoul knight ×2`, which reads as «there were two».
That is not what it counts: it counts **the ones that went down**. Now it says so
in those words.

The replay answers the other question —**how many there were**— and may give a
larger number, because it includes the one that never fell. Where both appear,
both are shown, each with its own word:

> `a shin ghoul knight` — 2 slain · at least 3 present

## Renaming your log no longer loses the hours after it

If you closed the game, renamed `eqlog_...txt` to start a fresh one, and played
for three hours before opening EQL Parse, **those three hours were never read**.
The program remembered which byte it had reached, the new file was shorter, and
it sat at the end without saying anything.

It now detects that the file is a different one and reads it from the start.

## And under the hood

- Quotes from other applications inside the help now read as quotes and not as
  our own text.
- A check has been added that runs over your entire history looking for things
  that **cannot be** —a death outside its own fight, someone active for more
  seconds than the fight lasted, a proportion greater than one—. There are
  seventeen of them today and all seventeen are silent.
